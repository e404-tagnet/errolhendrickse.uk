The Red Line & Signature

A defining element throughout Errol Hendrickse's work is the recurring presence of the red line, completed by his distinctive red signature. Neither is used as decoration. Together they form a continuous visual language that runs throughout the collection.

The red line appears in many forms, sometimes as a single uninterrupted gesture, sometimes fragmented, almost disappearing beneath layers of paint before resurfacing elsewhere. It is not intended to represent one fixed idea. Instead, it speaks of the invisible threads that connect us: memory, resilience, vulnerability, identity and the enduring human capacity to survive.

The red signature completes that journey. Rather than existing outside the painting as a mark of ownership, the signature is treated as the final red gesture. It belongs to the same visual language as the red line, becoming its conclusion rather than its addition.

The red line begins the conversation.The red signature brings it to rest.

About the Artist

Errol Hendrickse is a British contemporary artist whose paintings explore the intersections of memory, identity, psychological resilience and the human condition. Working at a granular level, he sculpts expressive abstraction and figurative works that investigate the fragile space between what is seen and what is experienced, creating paintings that speak to both personal and universal interpretation.

Hendrickse began his love of the creative arts in the nineties, learning to express himself through textile design. He developed an early appreciation for structure, rhythm and surface, then spent most of the 21st century working as a psychotherapist. He returned to painting as a way to express the sheer depth and variation of the human psyche, combined with the rich tapestry of lived experience.

His medium of choice is oil, charcoal and mixed media. He structures each work through a layered, reconstructed approach combined with mark-making, allowing every surface to retain the history and texture of its emotive state.

His abstract works are characterised by sweeping marks that give form to emotional states, thought and memory. His monochromatic collection explores very personal themes of vulnerable masculinity and emotional silence, while his most recent figurative style examines the individual and their sense of belonging and heritage.
